# What the shot gap measures

The source stores one persistent fact, its matching of physical ports. The random states are disposable. Every active link supplies a correlation in the exchange observable, while every port is individually maximally mixed on the full run. Measuring two ports jointly detects this correlation at dimension-independent strength. A common-basis receiver sees it as a small change in the rate of an already rare equality event.

The separation holds on the same emitted specimens. The receiver learns a classical wiring diagram from a mixture of product states across ports. The resource it uses is a joint quantum measurement across ports. Preparations within a port can be entangled across that register's qubits.

## Local storage and visibility

The full port-local lower bound allows temporal processing of every specimen received at a given port. The proof does not reduce that model to independent measurements. On the hard quartet family, classical communication still leaves ambiguous quartet assignments until enough correlation survives the partial-transpose bound. At fixed positive visibility this gives a sharp penalty proportional to the local dimension.

As visibility varies, the joint and specimen-separable scales remain sharp. The port-local lower and upper visibility dependences differ. Independent common-basis collisions attain the upper bound, and their optimality for the strongest port-local model remains open. Measurements that are positive under partial transpose across a cut include all local protocols across that cut and can be more permissive than actual local protocols.

## Receiver choices

A buffer holding all shots supports the minimum shot scaling. A round-robin schedule compares disjoint candidate edges in each shot and uses only a single coherent snapshot at a time. The [receiver chapter](receiver.md) charges both schedules for all emitted registers, even when their outcomes are unused. The upper bound uses ideal pair exchanges. In a concrete layout, moving registers, storing them through the sequence, and preserving a sufficient exchange contrast can change the engineering comparison.

The source promise is essential. The same latent state is shared within a shot, and fresh latent variables are independent across shots. If the preparation label is recorded and supplied, classical matching can use that side information. If the latent state is repeated across shots, separate ports can build estimates of it. Here the stable object is the wiring itself.

The joint threshold test uses only the two-port exchange contrast and independence across shots. With an independently justified margin for candidate links and nonlinks, the same test can recover a more general graph of exchange correlations. The matching model supplies a realizable family and sharp lower bounds on its own. Arbitrary graph promises can have additional compatibility and precision constraints.
